Your website has one job: help the right person decide you're the answer.

Not to list every service you've ever offered. Not to win a design award. Not to hold your full company history. Those things can live on a website. They just aren't its job.

When a site tries to do everything, visitors have to work to find what they need. People don't work that hard for a company they just met.

Here's what doing the one job looks like.

## Know who the right person is

"The right person" matters. Your website isn't for everyone. It's for the people you serve best.

Before you touch the design, finish one sentence: *Our website is for ____ who need ____.*

A commercial contractor might write, "facility managers who need a reliable builder for tenant improvements." A nonprofit might write, "local families looking for a safe after-school program, and the neighbors who want to support it."

Every decision after that gets easier. Does this page help that person? Keep it. Does it help no one in particular? Cut it or move it.

## Say what you do in the headline

The first words on your homepage carry the most weight. A visitor should read them and know three things: what you do, who it's for and why it matters.

Clever headlines often fail this test. "Building tomorrow, today" sounds nice and says nothing. "Commercial remodels for Indianapolis property owners" tells a facility manager to keep reading.

Read your headline out loud to someone who doesn't know your business. If they can't tell you what you do, rewrite it.

## Give every page one primary action

What should the right person do next? Call. Request a quote. Schedule a visit. Donate. Pick one, and make it the obvious next step.

That doesn't mean one button on the whole site. It means one primary action, repeated where it makes sense: near the top, after you've made your case and at the bottom. Secondary links can exist. They just shouldn't compete.

If your homepage has six buttons that all look equally important, the visitor has to choose. Make the choice for them.

## Show proof

People want to know you've done this before for someone like them. Tell them, then show them.

Proof can take a few forms:

- **Real work.** Photos, project descriptions or case studies.
- **Real words.** Testimonials from clients who have approved them.
- **Real names.** Logos of organizations you serve, used with permission.
- **Real people.** Your team's faces and roles.

Put proof near the decision. A short testimonial belongs next to the "Request a quote" button, not buried on a separate page.

Only use proof you can stand behind. An honest description of what you did will serve you longer than an inflated claim.

## Make it fast

A slow page can lose a visitor before they read a word. You don't need to be an engineer to check yours.

Open your site on your phone, on cellular data. Notice how long it takes before you can read the headline. If you're waiting, so are your visitors.

The usual culprits are oversized images, add-ons nobody uses and video that plays on its own. Compress your images. Remove what you don't use. Let video play when someone asks for it.

## Fix what's confusing

Every site has rough spots. A menu with vague labels. A contact form that asks for too much. A page nobody has updated in two years. A phone number you can't tap on a phone.

Find them by watching someone use your site. Ask a friend who fits your audience to request a quote or explain what you do. Don't help. Note where they hesitate.

Then fix those spots first. Small fixes often matter more than a redesign. Clearer labels, a shorter form and a tappable phone number cost little and remove real friction.

## Start with an honest audit

You don't always need a new website. Sometimes you need a clearer one. Start with five questions:

1. Can a stranger tell what we do at a glance?
2. Is there one obvious next step on every page?
3. Is there proof near that step?
4. Does it load quickly on a phone?
5. Where do people get stuck?

If the answer to most of these is no, it may be time to rebuild. If it's no to one or two, fix those and keep going.
